01 Why a year

Month four is where it starts to work.

The first few sessions of any consultation group are a performance. Everyone brings the case that makes them look thoughtful, the one with the tidy formulation and the client who is doing well. It is polite and it is useless.

Somewhere around the fourth month, if the group has stayed the same, somebody brings the other case. The one they have been avoiding. The one where they are not sure whether they have made a mistake or are about to. And the room, which by now knows them a little, does something a group of strangers cannot.

That is the whole argument for twelve months with the same eight people. Not that longer is better, but that trust has a lead time and most consultation groups are over before it arrives.

02

How it runs

Eight people, monthly

Small enough that everyone presents more than once a year and nobody can hide. The group is closed once it starts. If someone leaves, the seat stays empty rather than going to a stranger halfway through.

A facilitator, not a teacher

Someone from Full Bloom who still carries a caseload keeps it from turning into a lecture in one direction or a support group in the other. They bring cases too.

Any license, any setting

Private practice, agency, hospital, solo. Different settings in the same room has turned out to make the consultation sharper, because nobody can assume the others share their constraints.

03

A year, roughly

No two groups run the same way, but they tend to pass through the same weather.

  1. 1–3

    Settling. People bring the cases they are proud of. The facilitator asks more questions than anyone would like. Somebody says the thing about confidentiality out loud so that it has been said.

  2. 4–6

    The turn. Someone brings the case they have been avoiding, and the group handles it well, and after that the bar for what counts as a case worth bringing moves.

  3. 7–9

    The useful stretch. The group knows each other's caseloads and starts noticing patterns across them. This is the part that is hard to get anywhere else.

  4. 10–12

    Ending on purpose. Therapists know how to end things and rarely do it for themselves. The last two sessions are for that, and for deciding whether this group wants a second year.

04

Before you sign up

How often does the group meet, and for how long?

Monthly, for a full year, with the same eight people throughout. The length is deliberate. Somewhere around month four, people stop presenting their competence and start bringing the case that is keeping them up at night, and that does not happen in a group that reshuffles every quarter.

Do I need a particular license or setting?

No. Any license, any setting. Groups have had people from private practice, agencies, hospital systems and solo work in the same room, and the mix tends to make the consultation better rather than more diffuse.

Does it count as clinical supervision?

No. Consultation is between peers with a facilitator, and it is not supervision toward licensure. If you need supervised hours, that is a separate thing we also do, and the two can run alongside each other.

What does it cost?

Pricing goes to the waitlist first, when the opening cohort is scheduled. Two seats in every cohort are held at a reduced rate for clinicians in their first few years, working in community mental health, or in a stretch of year where the full fee isn’t happening. You email and ask.

What happens in a session?

One or two people bring a case. The group works it, with the facilitator keeping it from turning into either a lecture or a support group. What is said in the room stays there. By the end of the year the group knows your caseload well enough to notice what you are not saying.
