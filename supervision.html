01

Two ways to do the hours.

Supervision is the last structured place anyone will look closely at your work. We would rather it be useful than merely signed.

  1. i

    Individual. One supervisor, your caseload, the pace set by what is actually happening in your room this month rather than by a syllabus.

  2. ii

    Dyadic. Two supervisees, one supervisor. Less per person, and the other supervisee tends to ask the question you were too close to see.

  3. iii

    LPC and LMFT tracks in Colorado, with an AAMFT Approved Supervisor. If your track is something else, ask, and we will say plainly whether we can sign it.

02 Practice mentorship

The part nobody taught you.

You were trained to sit with a client. You were not trained to set a rate, raise it a year later, write a note that protects you without eating your evening, or decide whether taking insurance is worth what it costs you in paperwork and in autonomy.

Mentorship is for that. It can sit inside supervision, as the last twenty minutes of a session when the clinical work is done, or it can be its own thing for people who are already licensed and are building a practice without anyone to ask. The questions are practical and the answers are specific to Colorado and to how we run Full Bloom, which has been a group practice long enough to have got most of them wrong once.

03

How it starts

There is no intake form and no application. It begins with an email and a conversation, and most of the first conversation is us listening.

  1. One

    Write to us. Where you are in your hours, your license track, what setting you are in, and roughly what you are hoping supervision will be. Two paragraphs is plenty.

  2. Two

    A first conversation. Half an hour, no charge, to find out whether we are the right fit for the work you are doing. Sometimes the honest answer is that someone else would serve you better, and we will say so and point you there.

  3. Three

    A plan for the hours. How often, individual or dyadic, what the first three months will concentrate on, and what the paperwork looks like for your board. Then we start.

Openings are limited by how many supervisees one person can hold properly, so there is sometimes a wait. The waitlist hears first when a seat opens.

04

Who you would be working with

An AAMFT Approved Supervisor

The designation matters for LMFT candidates in particular, because not every licensed supervisor can sign those hours. Ours can, and has been doing it long enough to know where boards get picky.

Still in the chair

Your supervisor sees clients every week at Full Bloom. The advice you get about a difficult session is from someone who had one on Tuesday, not from memory of having had one in 2009.

A practice behind them

Full Bloom is a group practice with a referral network, a billing setup and a consultation habit. When a question comes up that your supervisor does not have the answer to, there are people down the hall who do.

05

Before you get in touch

Which licenses do you supervise toward?

LPC and LMFT hours in Colorado, individual and dyadic. Supervision is with an AAMFT Approved Supervisor. If you are working toward something else, email and ask, and we will tell you plainly whether we can sign off on it or point you to someone who can.

What is dyadic supervision?

Two supervisees with one supervisor. It costs less per person than individual, and in practice the second person is often the most useful part, because they will ask the question you were too close to the case to think of.

What is practice mentorship?

The part of the job that graduate school skipped. Setting rates and raising them, writing notes that protect you without taking your evening, building a referral network from nothing, deciding whether to take insurance, knowing when a client is outside your scope. It can be folded into supervision or booked on its own.

How do I start?

Email hello@therootandstem.com with where you are in your hours, your license track, and roughly what you are hoping for. We will tell you what is available and what it costs. There is no form to fill in first.

Can I do supervision and a consultation group at the same time?

Yes, and some people do. They are different things. Supervision is toward licensure, with someone responsible for your work. Consultation is between peers. Running both is more than most people need, but it is not more than some people want.
