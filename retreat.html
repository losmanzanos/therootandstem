The Root and Stem/Retreat

Retreats · Estes Park, Colorado

The person behind the therapist.

Three nights in the mountains with twelve to sixteen other clinicians. Teaching in the mornings, nothing scheduled in the afternoons, a long table in the evenings.

The first retreatThursday, November 4 to Sunday, November 7, 2027Estes Park, Colorado

Join the waitlist What the days look like
01

Therapists are excellent at prescribing rest.

We are dismal at taking it. This is built on the assumption that you already know what you need and haven’t had three consecutive days to go and find it.

It is not a training with a nicer view. There is teaching, and it is good, but it takes up the mornings and then stops. The afternoons are left empty on purpose, and we will not fill them if you look bored. Walk. Sleep. Sit by a window with a book that has nothing to do with the field.

The evenings are a long table. Meals are together and attendance is optional, and nobody will ask you to introduce yourself with a fun fact.

02

What the days look like

Three hours of teaching on two of the mornings. The rest is yours, and it stays yours.

  1. Thu

    Arrive in the afternoon. Settle in, find your room, find the kettle. Dinner together for anyone who wants it.

  2. Fri

    Teaching in the morning, three hours, one topic taken slowly, with room to argue with it. The afternoon is unscheduled.

  3. Sat

    Teaching in the morning, the second half of the same thread. Afternoon open. A long dinner.

  4. Sun

    Leave in the morning. Back in Denver by lunch, with the rest of the day still in front of you.

The topic and the person teaching it are set closer to the date and go to the waitlist first. Everyone who teaches here still carries a caseload, so the examples will be from this year, not from a slide deck that has been doing the rounds since 2014.

Far enough that the phone stops working properly. That is part of the point.
On choosing Estes Park
03

The practical part

Where

Estes Park, about an hour and a half from Denver. We are renting a house rather than block-booking a hotel, so it feels like staying somewhere, not checking in. Address and arrival details come with registration.

How many

Twelve to sixteen clinicians. Small enough that the people at dinner on Saturday are the same people you argued with on Friday morning, and you have somewhere to pick the thread back up.

Cost and seats

Pricing goes to the waitlist about eight weeks out, before it goes anywhere else. Two seats are held at a reduced rate for people early in their careers or in community mental health. No application, you just ask.

04

Before you sign up

Does the retreat carry continuing education credit?

It is built around rest rather than contact hours, so we are not planning to seek CE for it. If that changes, it will be stated on this page and in the email that opens registration. We would rather be plain about it than stretch a definition to make a weekend sound deductible.

How much does it cost, and when can I register?

Pricing and registration go to the waitlist first, about eight weeks before the dates, and the room is small enough that it may not reach a second announcement. Two seats are held at a reduced rate for clinicians in their first few years, working in community mental health, or in a stretch of year where the full fee isn’t happening. There is no application. You email and ask.

Who is it for?

Licensed therapists, pre-licensed clinicians accruing hours, and graduate students in a clinical program. You do not need any connection to Full Bloom. Most people who come to our rooms are from other practices, agencies, hospital systems or solo work.

How do I get to Estes Park?

It is about an hour and a half from Denver by car, up through Lyons or Boulder. Close enough to drive on a Thursday after your last client, far enough that your phone stops working properly, which is part of the point. Details on the house and arrival come with registration.

What is the cancellation policy?

Full refund up to 30 days before. Half up to 14 days. After that it becomes credit toward a future offering. Illness, a family emergency, a client crisis you can’t hand off: those get handled case by case, and we have never been rigid about it.
